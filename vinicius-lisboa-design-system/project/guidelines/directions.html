Recommendation: B

Direction B, with the accent held under roughly 2% of any surface. Three reasons.

  1. A is indistinguishable at thumbnail size. On a LinkedIn feed at 200px, pure monochrome editorial reads as a screenshot of a document. One slate rule and a cursive monogram are the only things in the system that survive that crop and says whose document it is.
  2. It gives emphasis somewhere to go. In A, every emphasis has to be made with weight, size or a heavier rule, which pushes the layouts louder over time. The accent absorbs that pressure and keeps the type quiet.
  3. It separates you from Klarun without breaking the family resemblance. Cool white ground plus blue-grey slate sits next to warm paper plus amber as a sibling, not a sub-brand — same restraint, opposite temperature.

Why slate #45617a

It is a grey with a light touch of blue — the same neutral family as the ink, pushed just far enough (hue 210, 27% saturation) to read as a colour rather than a darker grey. That is the whole point: the accent should feel like part of the greyscale, not an interruption of it, which is what a red could never do in a system this quiet. It is 6.5:1 on white and 5.4:1 (lifted to #8fa7bd) on the graphite ground, so it passes on both. It reproduces predictably in CMYK, in PowerPoint's picker and on a badly calibrated projector. And it is cool where Klarun's amber is warm — opposite temperature, same restraint, so the two sit side by side as siblings rather than as a set.

When to fall back to A

Keep Direction A in the back pocket for anything co-branded: a client deck, a Microsoft or Databricks event template, a slide that already carries Klarun. Apply data-direction="mono" to the root and the whole system goes accentless with no other change.
